# 0006 — Capacity and unit economics

- **Status:** Accepted (with open items)
- **Date:** 2026-09-07

## Context

The retail venture is supply-constrained by the dairy, and the retail margin is
thin. This decision records the numbers so the plan stays honest.

## Supply capacity

- Herd: **8–10 buffalo → ~70–80 kg/day maximum**, and only if the halwais are
  bypassed entirely.
- The halwais are the dairy's **core business**. Retail gets a **carve-out** of
  daily output, not the whole tank. Carve-out size is not yet agreed — this is
  part of the written supply deal (see 0002).
- We hold a **15–20 kg fridge buffer** (0002); unsold milk recirculates to the
  halwais, so it is not a loss.

## Demand vs. capacity

| Scenario | Households | Milk/day @ ~1–1.5 kg avg |
|---|---|---|
| Launch (15 regulars + a few casual) | ~15–20 | **~20–30 kg** |
| Stated near-term area | 50 | **50–75 kg** |

50 households ≈ the **entire herd's output**. It cannot coexist with the halwai
business at the current herd size. **50 is a medium-term ceiling, not a launch
number.** Reaching it needs more buffalo or a deliberate shift of volume away
from the halwais — a conversation to have with Aman & Manjeet.

## Unit economics at launch scale

- Margin: **₹60 sell − ₹55 cost = ₹5/kg** (~8%).
- 15 regulars × ~1.2 kg × ₹5 ≈ **₹90/day ≈ ₹2,700/month gross margin.**
- A salaried delivery boy is ₹3,000–6,000/month + fuel + packaging.
- **Net negative until roughly 40–50 kg/day of sales.**

## Decision

1. **At launch, the operator (or unpaid family) does the delivery.** No paid
   delivery boy until volume covers the wage — expected around **40–50 kg/day**.
   ("Delivery boy" in 0003 means whoever runs the round; a hired person is a
   later step.)
2. The **price step-up / delivery-fee trigger** (0002) is tied to the same
   threshold: revisit pricing when daily sales reach ~40–50 kg.
3. **Herd expansion is the real lever** for growth beyond ~50 kg/day. Flag it
   with the dairy early; don't sign up customers we can't supply.
