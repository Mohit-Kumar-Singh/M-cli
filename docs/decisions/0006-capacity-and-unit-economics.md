# 0006 — Capacity and unit economics

- **Status:** Accepted (revised 2026-09-09 — combined business)
- **Date:** 2026-09-07, revised 2026-09-09

## Supply capacity

- Herd: **8–10 buffalo → ~70–80 kg/day maximum.**
- Output is split between the two channels (0001): **wholesale (halwais)** and
  **retail (households)**. This is now a **channel allocation** decision each
  day, not an inter-company carve-out.
- A **15–20 kg fridge buffer** covers next-day retail demand spikes. Surplus
  milk goes to the halwai channel, which absorbs effectively unlimited
  quantity — so unsold retail stock is never a write-off. This is what makes
  the no-commitment retail offer safe (0005).

## Demand vs. capacity

| | Households | Retail milk/day @ ~1–1.5 kg avg |
|---|---|---|
| Launch (15 regulars + a few casual) | ~15–20 | **~20–30 kg** |
| Near-term retail area | 50 | **50–75 kg** |

50 retail households ≈ the whole herd's output, leaving nothing for wholesale —
so **50 is a medium-term ceiling, not a launch number** (accepted). This is an
initial-phase plan: prove retail works small, grow supply to match demand.

**Growth path for supply:**

1. Grow the fixed-household count gradually, within available milk.
2. Add buffalo to the herd.
3. Or source good milk from **vetted third-party vendors** and redistribute
   under the same quality standard (pure, unadulterated). Vendor vetting
   becomes the critical control.

Sequencing: add supply just ahead of demand; never sign customers we can't
supply.

## Unit economics

- One combined P&L (0001, 0002). Cost of milk = real cost of production.
- Retail margin at ₹60/kg is thin against production cost + delivery. At
  ~15 regulars (~₹90/day gross over a nominal ₹55 cost) it does not cover a
  delivery wage.
- **Therefore:** at launch the **operator (or unpaid family) runs the delivery
  round**. A paid delivery runner and the retail price step-up both wait until
  retail volume reaches ~**40–50 kg/day**. ("Delivery runner" in 0003/0007
  means whoever runs the round.)
